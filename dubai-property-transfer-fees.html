Dubai Property Transfer Fees: Complete Guide (2026)

Updated October 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory

When buying property in Dubai, the purchase price is only part of what you will pay. Transaction costs — led by the Dubai Land Department's 4% transfer fee — typically add 6–8% on top of the agreed price. This guide breaks down every charge, explains who pays what, and shows you the exact numbers at common price points.

The Dubai Land Department (DLD) transfer fee

The Dubai Land Department charges a 4% fee on the purchase price of every property transaction in Dubai. This applies to residential apartments, villas, townhouses and commercial property alike. It is the single largest transaction cost and cannot be avoided on secondary market purchases.

The 4% is calculated on the price stated in the sale contract (the Memorandum of Understanding, or MOU). The DLD monitors market values and has the authority to challenge contracts that appear significantly below market. In practice, declaring an artificially low price carries real risk — the DLD can reassess the value and levy the fee on a figure closer to market value.

Payment is made at the DLD trustee office on the day of transfer, using a manager's cheque. Both buyer and seller attend the trustee appointment to complete the title deed transfer.

Who pays? The 4% is almost always paid by the buyer in Dubai's secondary market. In some off-plan or corporate transactions, it is split 50/50 or covered by the developer. Whatever is agreed must be written into the MOU before you proceed — verbal agreements carry no legal weight at the trustee office.

What does the seller pay?

Sellers in Dubai face a much lighter cost burden than buyers:

  • No Objection Certificate (NOC): The seller obtains an NOC from the developer confirming no outstanding service charges or disputes. Cost: AED 500–5,000 depending on the developer. Larger, more prominent developers tend to charge more. This is non-negotiable — the transfer cannot proceed without it.
  • Mortgage discharge fee: If the property carries an existing mortgage, the seller must pay the bank's discharge fee to release the mortgage before transfer. This typically costs AED 1,050–1,575, and takes 2–5 working days from the bank.
  • Agency commission: If the seller's agent is charging a seller-side commission, it is typically 2% and paid at or before transfer. In practice, in Dubai's active market, agencies often earn from the buyer's side — but this is not universal.
  • No capital gains tax: Unlike many countries, there is no capital gains tax on property sales in Dubai. A seller who bought a property for AED 1M and sells for AED 1.5M has no tax liability on the AED 500K gain.
No property tax in Dubai: There is no annual property tax in Dubai. The ongoing ownership cost is the service charge — an annual maintenance fee paid to the building's management. Service charges range from AED 8–10 per sq ft in budget buildings to AED 40+ per sq ft in premium towers. This is separate from transfer fees and applies once you own the property.

Off-plan purchases: when the DLD fee is waived

One of the most significant financial benefits of buying off-plan directly from a developer in Dubai is that many developers offer to cover the 4% DLD fee as an incentive. This is particularly common during property launches, slower market periods, or for repeat investors.

On a AED 1.5M off-plan purchase, a DLD waiver saves you AED 60,000. That is a meaningful incentive, and it is one of the main reasons experienced investors pay close attention to which developers are offering fee waivers at any given time.

Major developers that have historically offered DLD fee waivers (subject to change by launch):

  • Emaar Properties — DLD waivers are common on selected launches, particularly for international investors
  • Damac Properties — frequently promotes DLD fee waivers as part of payment plan incentives
  • Aldar Properties — offers waivers selectively on key projects
  • Many boutique developers and mid-tier developers also offer waivers, particularly in a competitive launch environment
Critical rule: A developer's verbal promise to waive the DLD fee is worth nothing at the trustee office. The waiver must be explicitly stated in your Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA) or in a signed addendum. Always confirm this in writing before paying any deposit or signing any documents.

Even with a DLD fee waiver, off-plan buyers still pay the registration trustee fee (AED 5,250 for properties above AED 500K) at handover when the unit is transferred to their name. Factor this in.

Family transfers: reduced 0.125% fee

When property is transferred as a gift between first-degree family members — specifically between parents and children, or between spouses — the DLD applies a reduced transfer fee of 0.125% instead of 4%. This represents a substantial saving and makes Dubai's property regime very favourable for inter-generational wealth transfer.

Example: A property worth AED 5 million transferred as a gift from a parent to a child would incur a DLD fee of AED 6,250 (0.125%) rather than AED 200,000 (4%). The saving is almost AED 194,000.

To qualify, the relationship must be documented (passport and family records), and the transfer must be structured as a genuine gift with no commercial consideration. A UAE-licensed property lawyer can advise on structuring and documentation requirements.

Mortgage registration and valuation fees

If you are financing your purchase through a UAE bank mortgage, there are two additional DLD fees to budget for:

Mortgage registration fee

The DLD charges 0.25% of the mortgage amount, plus AED 290 to register the mortgage. This is paid on the day of transfer at the trustee office alongside the transfer fee. On a AED 1,000,000 mortgage, the registration fee is AED 2,500 + AED 290 = AED 2,790.

Mortgage valuation fee

Before the bank approves your mortgage, they require an independent valuation of the property to confirm it is worth what you are paying. Valuation fees in Dubai are typically AED 2,500–3,500 depending on the bank and the property. The valuation must be carried out by a DLD-approved valuer — your bank will direct you to their approved panel.

Bank processing and arrangement fees

Most UAE banks also charge a mortgage processing or arrangement fee of 0.5–1% of the loan amount when the mortgage is issued. This is separate from the DLD fees above and goes directly to the bank. Some banks waive or reduce this for certain products or promotions — worth comparing when selecting your lender.

Can you negotiate who pays the fees?

The 4% DLD fee is a legal charge and its existence is not negotiable — but who pays it can be. In a buyer's market or when a seller is highly motivated, it is reasonable to ask the seller to contribute to the DLD fee. A motivated seller might agree to pay 1–2% of the DLD fee, effectively sharing the cost.

Agency commissions are also negotiable in principle, though agencies are less likely to discount them in an active market. In a slower market, some agencies will work for 1.5% or even 1% on larger transactions.

Whatever is agreed must be written into the MOU before you sign. The key points to document:

  • Which party is responsible for the DLD transfer fee
  • Which party is responsible for the trustee fee
  • The exact agency commission amount and who pays it
  • Any developer DLD fee waiver (for off-plan)

Frequently asked questions

Is the 4% DLD fee negotiable?

The fee itself is set by law — 4% is non-negotiable. Who pays it is negotiable. By convention, the buyer pays, but in practice it can be split or covered by a developer. Always get the agreed arrangement in writing.

Do I pay the DLD fee on the full mortgage value or just my equity?

The 4% DLD transfer fee is calculated on the full purchase price, not just your equity or deposit. If you buy a property for AED 2,000,000 with a 25% deposit and 75% mortgage, you pay DLD on AED 2,000,000 (AED 80,000), not just on the deposit of AED 500,000.

When exactly is the DLD fee paid?

The DLD transfer fee is paid on the day both parties attend the DLD trustee office to complete the transfer of title. You will need a manager's cheque made out to the Dubai Land Department for the full 4% amount. The trustee will not process the transfer without it.

Is there any VAT on the DLD fee?

Residential property transactions in Dubai are exempt from VAT. Commercial property transactions are subject to 5% VAT. The 4% DLD fee itself does not attract additional VAT for residential property.

What is the trustee office fee, and is it different from the DLD fee?

Yes — these are two separate charges. The DLD transfer fee (4%) goes to the Dubai Land Department as a government charge. The trustee office fee (AED 4,000 or AED 5,250) is paid to the private trustee office (a DLD-registered intermediary) for processing the paperwork and completing the transfer. Both are paid on transfer day.

Do I pay transfer fees on off-plan property at purchase or at handover?

For off-plan property bought directly from a developer, the DLD fee and registration trustee fee are typically paid at the time of initial sale registration — shortly after you sign the SPA and pay your initial deposit. If the developer is offering a DLD waiver, the fee is waived at this stage. The property is registered in your name with the DLD as an off-plan sale (under Oqood registration), which costs approximately AED 3,000–4,000. When the building is completed and the unit is transferred to full title, a further trustee fee may apply.

How does Dubai compare to other countries on transaction costs?

Dubai's ~6–7% total transaction cost for buyers is roughly comparable to the UK (Stamp Duty + fees), higher than some Gulf markets, but broadly competitive by international standards. What distinguishes Dubai is the complete absence of annual property tax, capital gains tax and inheritance tax — costs that affect total ownership returns significantly over a holding period. A buyer in the UK, for example, pays Stamp Duty Land Tax of up to 12% on the purchase, then Capital Gains Tax on sale. In Dubai, the 4% is a one-time fee with no further tax burden.
