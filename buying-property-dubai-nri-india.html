Buying Property in Dubai as an Indian NRI: Complete Guide (2026)

Updated November 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory

Indians are consistently among the top-3 nationalities buying property in Dubai. There are no special NRI restrictions, no extra taxes, and no ownership caps. If you are an Indian NRI or resident Indian researching Dubai real estate, this guide covers everything: the legal framework, the buying process, FEMA rules, costs, mortgage options, and which agencies specialise in Indian buyers.

Can an Indian NRI buy property in Dubai?

Yes — without restriction. Dubai's freehold property law, introduced in 2002, allows foreign nationals of any country to purchase freehold ownership in designated freehold zones. There is no nationality-based restriction, no special approval required for Indians, no NRI-specific quota, and no additional tax on foreign buyers.

Indians do not need UAE residency to buy property in Dubai. Non-resident buyers are welcome and the entire transaction can be completed remotely — including KYC verification, document review, and payment transfer — without travelling to Dubai.

As of 2025, Indians ranked among the top-3 buyer nationalities in Dubai, alongside British and Russian nationals. The Indian buyer base includes NRIs resident in the UAE, NRIs in other countries (UK, US, Singapore, Canada), and India-resident buyers making overseas investments.

Resident Indians vs NRIs: Both can buy Dubai property. India-resident individuals purchasing overseas property are subject to RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) limit of USD 250,000 per financial year per individual. NRIs (resident abroad) have no LRS restriction on remitting from their overseas income or NRE accounts.

Freehold zones and ownership rights

Foreigners (including Indians) can only purchase freehold property in designated freehold zones established under Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006. Outside these zones, foreigners can hold leasehold interests (typically 99-year leases) but not freehold title.

The major freehold zones cover most of the areas where Indian buyers typically invest:

  • Dubai Marina and JLT
  • Downtown Dubai and Business Bay
  • Palm Jumeirah
  • Dubai Hills Estate
  • Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC) and Jumeirah Village Triangle (JVT)
  • Arabian Ranches, The Springs, The Meadows, Emirates Hills
  • Creek Harbour, Meydan, and Mohammed Bin Rashid City
  • Dubailand zones and Al Furjan

Freehold ownership in Dubai is registered with the Dubai Land Department and evidenced by a Title Deed (Oqood for off-plan). There is no expiry date — it is permanent ownership equivalent in structure to English freehold title.

The buying process step by step

  1. Engage a RERA-registered agent Find an agent who specialises in your target area and property type. For international buyers, agencies with multilingual teams are worth prioritising. Verify the agent's RERA card number at dubailand.gov.ae before proceeding.
  2. Search and shortlist Browse available units through your agent. For secondary market (ready), you can physically visit or request a video walkthrough. For off-plan, review floor plans, the developer's SPA, and comparable projects.
  3. Agree price and sign Form F (MOU) When you agree on a price with the seller, the RERA Form F (Memorandum of Understanding) is signed. At this stage you typically pay a 10% deposit, held in trust or in the developer's escrow account. The deposit is refundable only if the seller defaults.
  4. Obtain No Objection Certificate (NOC) For secondary market transactions, the seller must obtain a NOC from the building's developer or Owners Association confirming there are no outstanding liabilities on the property (unpaid service charges, mortgage). This takes 5–15 working days.
  5. DLD transfer appointment Buyer and seller (or their authorised representatives under Power of Attorney) attend the Dubai Land Department's trustee office to complete the title transfer. The buyer pays the remaining purchase price, 4% DLD transfer fee, AED 580 registration fee, and receives the new Title Deed. This can be done on the same day as the NOC is received.
  6. Collect keys and register utilities After the DLD transfer, the seller hands over keys and access cards. DEWA (electricity and water) and du/Etisalat (internet) accounts are transferred or created in the buyer's name.

FEMA rules and remittance

For NRIs funding the purchase from overseas accounts or income, the process is straightforward:

  • NRE account holders can remit funds freely from their NRE account to pay for overseas property. NRE funds are fully repatriable without RBI restrictions.
  • NRO account holders can remit up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO accounts after paying applicable Indian taxes and obtaining a CA certificate (Form 15CB/15CA).
  • FCNR(B) account holders can break an FCNR deposit and remit proceeds overseas freely.

For India-resident buyers (not NRIs), the LRS limit of USD 250,000 per financial year per person applies. A married couple can jointly remit USD 500,000 in a single year. Property purchased under LRS cannot be sold and the proceeds directly reinvested in another overseas property without routing the funds back to India first.

Tax on repatriation: Proceeds from selling Dubai property (capital gains) are not taxed in Dubai. When repatriating proceeds to India, NRIs should consult a tax advisor regarding Indian capital gains tax treatment on foreign assets, TDS obligations, and Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) provisions between India and the UAE.

Mortgage options for NRIs

NRIs can obtain mortgages from UAE banks as well as some Indian banks for Dubai property purchases:

UAE banks (for Dubai property)

Emirates NBD, ADCB, Mashreq, HSBC UAE, and Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank (ADIB) all offer mortgages to non-resident foreign nationals for Dubai property purchases. Typical terms:

  • Loan-to-value: up to 75% for first property (25% minimum down payment)
  • Maximum tenure: 25 years
  • Interest rates: approximately 4.5–6.5% per annum (variable with EIBOR base)
  • Proof of income, bank statements, and passport/visa documentation required

Indian banks (NRI home loans)

HDFC Bank, SBI, ICICI Bank, and Bank of Baroda offer NRI home loans for overseas property in select countries including the UAE. These are funded in Indian rupees and secured against the foreign property — less common for Dubai transactions but an option for NRIs who prefer to bank with Indian institutions.

Rental income and taxation

Dubai imposes no tax on rental income earned from property in the UAE. As a property owner, you receive 100% of your net rental income after agent fees, service charges, and any maintenance costs — no income tax deduction at source in Dubai.

For NRI landlords, the Indian tax position depends on residential status under FITA (Income Tax Act). An NRI's foreign income is not taxable in India. However, if the NRI has also received income in India in the same year, a CA's advice on correct filing is advisable. The India-UAE DTAA provides further protection against double taxation.

Dubai Golden Visa for property buyers

Purchasing Dubai property above AED 2 million entitles the buyer to apply for a 10-year UAE Golden Visa. For NRIs considering Dubai as a base, the Golden Visa is a significant benefit — it provides long-term residency without employer sponsorship and extends to the buyer's spouse and children.

Key points for Indian buyers seeking the Golden Visa via property:

  • Property must be completed and registered in the buyer's name (off-plan Oqood qualifies if the property value meets the threshold)
  • The 10-year visa is renewable and does not require continuous UAE residence
  • Indian passport holders holding a UAE Golden Visa can open UAE bank accounts freely, reducing the friction of international money transfers for future purchases

Popular areas for Indian buyers

Indian buyers in Dubai typically concentrate in a handful of areas, partly driven by existing community presence and partly by value-for-money considerations:

  • Downtown Dubai and Business Bay — flagship addresses, strong rental demand, familiar Indian community presence
  • Dubai Marina and JBR — waterfront lifestyle, strong short-term rental market, easy access
  • Jumeirah Village Circle — highest-yielding apartments in Dubai for the price point, popular with NRI investors seeking rental income
  • Dubai Hills Estate — family destination, school access, greenery; popular with NRIs planning to live in Dubai
  • Palm Jumeirah — premium investment and lifestyle destination; established Indian community in several developments
