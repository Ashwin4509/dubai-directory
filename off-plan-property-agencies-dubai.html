What off-plan means in Dubai

Off-plan property is property sold before construction is complete — sometimes before the first brick is laid. You buy at today's price and pay in instalments tied to construction milestones. On completion, you take ownership of a finished unit.

In Dubai, the off-plan market is regulated by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) under the Dubai Land Department. Developers must hold buyer payments in an escrow account that can only be drawn down as construction milestones are verified. This is a meaningful protection — Dubai off-plan is considerably better regulated than most comparable markets.

The main reasons buyers choose off-plan: lower entry prices than comparable ready units, extended payment plans (60/40, 50/50, post-handover plans of two to five years), and the potential for capital appreciation between contract and handover. The main risks: construction delays, developer insolvency (less common post-2008 regulatory reforms), and the possibility that the completed product differs from the show apartment.

Before any off-plan purchase, verify the project is registered with RERA and that the developer holds an active escrow account. Your agent can confirm both. Do not transfer money to a developer whose escrow account cannot be verified.

What to look for in an off-plan agency

Not all agencies have equal access to off-plan inventory. Developers maintain approved broker networks and give priority allocation — and sometimes pre-launch pricing — to brokers who move significant volume with them. The following things are worth asking about before you engage:

  • Developer registration: Is the broker a registered agent with the developers you are interested in? Ask which developers they have active relationships with.
  • Pre-launch access: Can they show you units before public launch? Pre-launch pricing is typically 5–15% below the public launch price and is only available through registered brokers with existing developer relationships.
  • Payment plan knowledge: Off-plan payment plans vary significantly between developers — some are construction-linked, others are time-based, and some include post-handover periods. An experienced off-plan broker will know the differences and help you compare total cash exposure across options.
  • Post-sale support: The relationship does not end at signing. Construction updates, snagging inspections, and handover coordination are things a good off-plan agency will assist with.

Questions to ask before buying off-plan

These are the questions worth raising with any agent before committing to an off-plan purchase:

  • Is the project registered with RERA? Ask for the project registration number and verify it at the DLD portal.
  • Who is the escrow bank? All off-plan projects must have a RERA-approved escrow account. Payments go to the escrow bank, not the developer directly.
  • What is the developer's delivery track record? Ask for three recent completed projects and their actual versus promised handover dates. Most major developers have this information publicly available.
  • What does the payment plan look like at each milestone? Get the full payment schedule in writing before you sign anything.
  • What are the service charges after handover? RERA publishes approved service charge rates. High service charges erode rental yield — check before you buy.
  • Is the unit you are buying the same as the show apartment? Show apartments typically include upgrades not included in the standard spec. Ask what the standard finish includes.
