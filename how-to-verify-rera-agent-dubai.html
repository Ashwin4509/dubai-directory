How to Verify a Dubai Real Estate Agent is RERA Registered (2026)

What is RERA and who must be registered?

RERA — the Real Estate Regulatory Agency — is the regulator for the Dubai property market. It is a division of the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and is responsible for licensing all real estate professionals and companies operating in Dubai.

Under UAE law, every person who facilitates, brokers or advises on a Dubai property transaction must hold a valid RERA broker card. This covers:

  • Individual sales agents (the person showing you around a property)
  • Letting agents (managing or brokering rental agreements)
  • Property management companies (managing units on behalf of owners)
  • Real estate agencies as corporate entities
  • Off-plan sales agents working for developer marketing teams

Operating as a real estate broker without a valid RERA card is a criminal offence in Dubai, subject to fines and legal action. Despite this, unregistered agents do operate — particularly in the rental market — and buyers and tenants who transact with them have no regulatory protection if something goes wrong.

Verification is the single most important piece of due diligence you can do before agreeing to anything with a Dubai real estate agent.

RERA card numbers are on business cards for a reason: Legitimate RERA-registered agents print their RERA broker card number on their business cards, email signatures and marketing materials. If you can't find a card number, treat that as an immediate flag and ask directly before proceeding.

How to verify RERA registration: step by step

There are two official routes to verify RERA registration. Both are free, official and take under two minutes.

Method 1: Dubai REST app (fastest)

Dubai REST is the official app from the Dubai Land Department, available on iOS and Android. It is the quickest way to verify an individual agent's RERA card status.

1

Download Dubai REST

Search for "Dubai REST" on the App Store or Google Play. The official app is published by the Dubai Land Department. It is free.

2

Open "Brokers" or use the search

From the home screen, navigate to the services section and select "Real Estate Services" or search for "broker verification". The DLD broker directory is accessible without creating an account.

3

Enter the agent's name or card number

Search using the agent's full name or their RERA card number (usually a 5–6 digit number). Both searches work. If the agent has given you their business card, enter the number printed on it.

4

Check the result

A valid result shows the broker's name, their RERA card number, their registered employer (the agency they work for), and the card's expiry date. Confirm the card is current — not expired — and that the employer matches the agency introducing you.

Method 2: DLD website broker search

The Dubai Land Department's website (dubailand.gov.ae) also has a public broker verification tool. Navigate to the "Real Estate Services" section, then find the "Broker Verification" or "Register Inquiry" tools. You can search by name or RERA registration number. This is useful if you are on a desktop computer and don't have the Dubai REST app to hand.

Method 3: Ask to see the physical RERA card

RERA cards are physical ID cards issued to registered brokers. Any agent who has completed RERA certification and paid their annual registration fee will have one. Asking an agent to show you their card, or to photograph the card number for you, is a completely standard and reasonable request. No legitimate agent will object to this.

Expiry date matters: RERA broker cards expire annually and must be renewed. A card that expired six months ago is no longer valid — the agent is technically operating without authorisation until they renew. Always check the expiry date, not just that a card exists.

What RERA registration records show you

When you look up a broker in the DLD system, the record typically includes:

FieldWhat it tells you
Broker nameThe registered individual's full name — verify it matches who you're dealing with
RERA card numberTheir unique registration number, printed on their physical card
Registration statusActive / Inactive / Suspended — only "Active" is valid
Registered employerThe agency this broker is authorised to work for — must match the agency introducing you
Card expiry dateRERA cards are annual; check this is in the future
License categorySome brokers are licensed only for sales, others for leasing too — check it covers your transaction type

The registered employer field is particularly important. An agent whose RERA card shows them employed by Agency X is not authorised to broker transactions on behalf of Agency Y. This matters if an agent is working across multiple companies or has recently changed employers — their card must reflect their current employer.

Agency (company) registration vs broker (individual) registration

RERA operates two separate registers — one for companies (agencies) and one for individual brokers. Both matter, and both should be checked:

Agency (company) RERA registration

Every real estate company operating in Dubai must hold a DLD-issued real estate brokerage licence. This is separate from and in addition to their general UAE trade licence. You can verify a company's licence by searching the DLD's broker registry for the company name, or by checking Dubai REST under "Service Providers" or "Real Estate Companies".

A legitimate agency will have a DLD licence number (often called a "DLD brokerage number" or "RERA registration number for companies") that they should be willing to share on request. Many display it on their website and email footers.

Individual broker RERA card

Separately, every individual working as a broker must hold a personal RERA broker card. The card is specific to that person and their employer. So even if the agency is RERA-registered, the individual agent you are dealing with must also have their own valid card.

Both checks are required. An agency with a valid company licence can still employ unlicensed individuals — which is illegal but does occur. Checking the individual's card closes this gap.

RERA certification training: To obtain a RERA broker card, individuals must complete a RERA-certified training course (typically offered by the Dubai Real Estate Institute, DREI) and pass an assessment. The card is then issued and must be renewed annually with continued professional development. This training is what distinguishes a licensed broker from someone who is merely employed by an agency.

Red flags: signs an agent may not be properly registered

Here are the most common warning signs that the person you're dealing with may not hold a valid RERA registration:

  • No RERA card number on their business card or marketing materials. Every registered broker should display their number. Its absence is a strong flag.
  • They refuse or deflect when you ask for their card number. Vague responses like "I'll check with the office" or "my card is at the office" are red flags. A registered broker always knows their own card number.
  • The name in the DLD system doesn't match the person you're meeting. Always cross-reference the name on the card with the person in front of you.
  • The card is registered to a different employer than the agency they're introducing themselves as. This can indicate the agent recently moved companies and hasn't updated their RERA registration — which is their responsibility to do promptly.
  • The card has expired. An expired card means the agent is not currently authorised to broker transactions. Some agents work for months before renewing. This is not uncommon and not necessarily malicious, but it is non-compliant.
  • They operate purely via WhatsApp with no company identity. Some unregistered agents operate informally and avoid providing verifiable documentation. Any agent unwilling to provide a business card, company name and DLD licence number should be treated with significant caution.
  • Pressure to pay a "booking fee" or "admin fee" before viewing. This is not standard practice and is associated with scam operations — RERA-registered agents do not charge viewing or booking fees before a formal MOU is signed.

What to do if an agent cannot prove registration

If an agent cannot or will not provide a verifiable RERA card number, your best course of action is to stop the transaction until the matter is resolved. Specifically:

  • Do not sign any documents. An MOU, tenancy contract or booking form signed with an unregistered broker has no RERA protection and may be legally problematic.
  • Do not pay any deposit or fee. Any money transferred to an unregistered agent is essentially unsecured and not covered by RERA's escrow or dispute resolution frameworks.
  • Contact the agency directly. If the agent claims to work for a named agency, call that agency's main office and ask to verify the agent's employment and RERA status before proceeding.
  • Report to RERA if you believe someone is operating without a licence. RERA has a complaints hotline and an online reporting mechanism. Reporting unregistered brokers helps protect other buyers and tenants.

If you need to proceed quickly with a transaction, the safest option is to work directly with an established, clearly RERA-registered agency. Major agencies like Betterhomes, fäm Properties, Allsopp & Allsopp and haus & haus all publish their DLD licence numbers and ensure every agent they employ holds a current RERA card.

Quick verification checklist

Before you proceed with any Dubai real estate transaction, run through this checklist:

  • ☐ Ask the agent for their RERA card number (or look for it on their business card / email signature)
  • ☐ Look up the number on Dubai REST or dubailand.gov.ae — confirm status is "Active"
  • ☐ Confirm the expiry date is in the future
  • ☐ Confirm the registered employer matches the agency introducing you
  • ☐ Verify the agency itself has a DLD brokerage licence (ask for their company DLD number)
  • ☐ Cross-reference the name on the RERA record with the person you are meeting
  • ☐ If buying: confirm the property is listed on Bayut or Dubizzle (properties marketed without a RERA permit number on portals are non-compliant)
Permit numbers on property listings: RERA requires all property advertisements — on portals like Property Finder, Bayut and Dubizzle, as well as printed flyers — to include the agent's RERA card number and the property's specific advertising permit number. If a listing you are responding to does not show these numbers, the advertisement itself may be non-compliant, which is a warning sign about the agent's practices.
