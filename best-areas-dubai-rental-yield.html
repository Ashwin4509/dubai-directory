Rental yield overview: how Dubai compares globally

Gross rental yield is the annual rent divided by the purchase price, expressed as a percentage. It is the first number investors use to compare markets and assets. In Dubai, gross yields in mid-market communities typically run 6–9% — meaningfully higher than London (3–4%), Singapore (2–3%) or Sydney (3–4%).

Several structural factors sustain Dubai's yields. There is no annual property tax, so the gap between gross and net yield is narrower than in most Western markets (the main recurring cost is the annual service charge). The city's economy continues to attract a large expatriate workforce that rents by default — homeownership rates remain low relative to population, keeping tenant demand strong. And population growth has consistently exceeded supply growth in mid-market communities.

That said, gross yield is not the full picture. What you actually earn depends on vacancy rate, service charges, management fees and financing costs. This guide focuses on gross yields, but we flag the factors that push net yields lower for each area.

Key terms: Gross yield = annual rent ÷ purchase price. Net yield = (annual rent − service charges − management fees − vacancy cost) ÷ purchase price. In Dubai, the gap is typically 1.5–3 percentage points depending on the building and your management approach.

Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC)

JVC is Dubai's most consistent high-yield community. It offers a compelling combination: a mid-market price bracket that most investors can access, a large and diverse tenant pool, and yields that have held in the 7–9% range for several years even as prices have risen.

Why JVC delivers strong yields

The community is roughly 20 minutes from DIFC and Downtown by car, making it accessible to the professional workforce that drives Dubai's rental demand. Rents are affordable relative to Marina or Downtown, which keeps occupancy high — well-presented apartments let quickly, often within 2–4 weeks of vacancy. The community has matured significantly since 2020: retail, clinics, cafes and schools have filled in, reducing the "incomplete development" discount that suppressed yields earlier.

What to watch out for in JVC

Service charges in JVC vary considerably. A newer tower with a gym, pool and concierge might charge AED 15–22 per square foot per year; an older building might charge AED 10–14. On a 700 sq ft apartment, that's the difference between AED 7,000 and AED 15,400 per year coming off your rent. Check the RERA service charge register (available via the Dubai REST app) before buying, not after.

JVC also has a large and growing off-plan pipeline. As new towers hand over, they add supply and can soften rents temporarily in specific micro-pockets of the community. Focus on established sub-communities — JVC Circle Districts and the central areas — rather than the outer fringes where handover risk is higher.

Typical JVC numbers (2026): 1BR apartment at AED 900K, annual rent AED 72K = 8% gross. After service charge (AED 12K) and management fee (8% = AED 5,760), net yield ≈ 6%. Still strong by global standards.

Business Bay

Business Bay sits immediately south of Downtown Dubai on the Dubai Canal. It has transformed from a stalled commercial district into one of Dubai's most dynamic mixed-use communities. The Dubai Canal promenade, a growing restaurant scene, and proximity to DIFC make it attractive to young professionals and corporate tenants — the demographic that pays on time and renews leases.

Why Business Bay yields remain attractive

Business Bay offers something that JVC cannot: a walkable urban environment with the Dubai Canal as a backdrop. Tenants pay a premium for this — rents have risen faster than purchase prices in some segments, compressing yields from their highs but keeping them in the 6–8% range for well-priced 1BR and 2BR apartments. Buildings with direct canal views command higher rents and also hold value more reliably in a downturn.

The commercial element of Business Bay (office tenants, hotels, serviced apartments) generates a steady stream of corporate housing requirements. If your apartment is well-furnished and in a managed building, short-term rental through platforms like Airbnb can push effective yield above 10% — though this requires a holiday home licence from DTCM and active management.

Business Bay considerations

Service charges in Business Bay are higher on average than in JVC — expect AED 18–28 per square foot for most towers, reflecting the facilities and location premium. Traffic congestion around Sheikh Zayed Road intersections during peak hours is a consistent complaint from tenants. Buildings without direct canal views can feel hemmed in, so position matters for sustained rental demand.

Dubai Marina

Dubai Marina is one of the world's largest man-made marinas and a defining address in Dubai real estate. It consistently ranks among the city's most popular areas for both buyers and renters. As an investment, however, it represents a different proposition to JVC or Business Bay: lower gross yield, but stronger long-term capital growth and deep liquidity.

Marina yield profile

Gross yields for Marina apartments typically range 5.5–7% — below the Dubai average for mid-market communities, but on par with better-quality urban districts in other global cities. The key advantage is the breadth of tenant demand: Marina appeals to a wide range of expatriates — from young professionals in studios to families in large 3BR apartments near the beach. Vacancy is low, and renewal rates are high because tenants place genuine lifestyle value on the address.

Marina also benefits from the JBR (Jumeirah Beach Residence) effect: beachfront access, the Walk, and proximity to Dubai Eye create a demand anchor that more inland communities lack. This is why Marina continues to attract buyers even at yields below 6% — they are buying into a proven, liquid market where capital preservation is more certain.

When Marina makes sense

  • You want a property that is easy to sell later (Marina is one of Dubai's most traded communities)
  • You are buying a 2BR or 3BR apartment for a mix of family rental and eventual own-use
  • You want to run a holiday home — Marina properties command strong short-term rental premiums in peak season
  • You are prioritising capital growth alongside yield
Marina vs JVC — the tradeoff: On a AED 1.5M budget, Marina gives you a dated 1BR with 5.5% yield and strong capital growth potential. JVC gives you a newer 2BR with 7.5% yield and moderate capital growth. Neither is wrong — they suit different investor profiles.

Arjan and Dubailand

Arjan is a sub-community within Dubailand, adjacent to Al Barsha and roughly 10 minutes from Mall of the Emirates. It is one of Dubai's fastest-improving areas for yield investors: entry prices remain below AED 1 million for a 1BR, the area has seen significant new retail and infrastructure investment, and rents have risen faster than prices in the past two years.

The tenant base skews towards young professionals and couples relocating from more expensive areas like Dubai Marina — a "spillover" effect that sustains demand. Proximity to Dubai British School and other educational institutions has also started attracting small family tenants, broadening the pool beyond just young professionals.

The main risk in Arjan is the same as in any emerging area: a heavy off-plan pipeline that can add supply quickly. Research the number of units currently under construction before buying, and prefer established buildings with a track record of occupancy over new towers that have just handed over.

Dubai Sports City

Dubai Sports City offers some of the most affordable entry prices in Dubai for investors chasing yield — 1BR apartments from AED 600,000, with gross yields regularly above 7.5%. The community benefits from steady institutional demand: employees of media city, sports academies and the several schools within or adjacent to the area provide a reliable tenant flow.

The area's challenge is accessibility. It sits relatively far from DIFC and Downtown, and the absence of Metro connectivity means car-dependent tenants are the core market. Demand from public-transport-only tenants is minimal. That said, within its target tenant profile, vacancy rates have been low — the right tenant finds good value here and tends to stay.

For investors with a budget under AED 800,000 seeking maximum yield, Dubai Sports City alongside JVC and Arjan is worth serious consideration.

Discovery Gardens

Discovery Gardens is Dubai's most affordable established community for yield investors. Studios and 1BR apartments can be acquired from AED 400,000–700,000, and rental yields in the 7–8.5% range are achievable on well-priced units. The community is mature — it has been fully developed for over 15 years — with established retail, dining and transport links.

The tenant base is predominantly lower-to-middle-income professionals and blue-collar workers, which means lower rents but also consistent demand. Vacancy is rare in Discovery Gardens. The main downside is capital appreciation: prices in Discovery Gardens have grown more slowly than in JVC or Business Bay, so investors who optimise purely for yield do better here than investors who also want capital growth.

Investors with larger budgets or longer horizons can afford to weight capital growth more heavily.

What reduces your actual yield

Gross yield headlines are useful for comparison, but several factors eat into what you actually receive:

  • Service charges: Dubai's RERA-regulated annual service charge covers building maintenance, security, shared facilities and insurance. In mid-market buildings this is typically AED 10–20 per sq ft; in premium towers AED 25–40. On a 700 sq ft apartment, that is AED 7,000–28,000 per year. Always check the RERA service charge register before buying — not all agents volunteer this information upfront.
  • Property management fees: If you use a management company (highly recommended for absentee investors), expect to pay 7–10% of annual rent. On AED 80K rent, that is AED 5,600–8,000 per year. Some agencies offer a fixed fee rather than a percentage — worth comparing for expensive units.
  • Vacancy: Even in strong markets, there will be periods between tenancies. Budget for 2–4 weeks of vacancy per year, plus periodic maintenance between tenants.
  • Maintenance and repairs: Budget AED 3,000–8,000 per year for appliance repairs, minor works and periodic repainting between tenancies. Older buildings and cheaper appliances mean higher maintenance costs.
  • Mortgage financing costs: If you are buying on mortgage, your net yield needs to cover the mortgage cost. At current UAE mortgage rates (typically 5–7% for non-residents), a highly leveraged investment can easily run cash-flow negative in the first years.
Net yield rule of thumb: In Dubai, deduct 1.5–2.5% from gross yield to estimate net yield in a well-managed, fully let mid-market property. A 7.5% gross yield typically nets 5–6% after service charges and management fees — still very competitive globally, but set your expectations correctly from the outset.

Which agencies are best for investment buyers

Not every agency in Dubai is well set up to serve yield-focused investors. The agencies that add most value for investment buyers are those with:

  • Community-level data: Specific recent sales and rental transactions in your target area, not just general market figures
  • Post-purchase management: Either in-house property management or strong referral relationships with management companies
  • Off-plan expertise (if relevant): Access to developer allotments and the ability to advise on payment plan structures
