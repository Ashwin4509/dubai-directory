Dubai Marina: Property Guide for Buyers & Investors (2026)

Updated October 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory editorial team

Dubai Marina is the city's most recognisable waterfront address — 3.5 km of man-made canal lined with over 200 residential towers, a promenade of restaurants and retail, and direct access to Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR). It is one of Dubai's most active property markets for both resale buyers and investors seeking rental income, and it is a designated freehold area open to all nationalities.

Area overview

Dubai Marina was developed by Emaar Properties in the early 2000s on a strip of reclaimed land adjacent to the Arabian Gulf. It is now one of the most densely populated residential districts in the UAE. The marina canal is navigable by boat and flanked by the Marina Walk promenade, which connects most of the major towers at ground level.

The area is well served by public transport: two Dubai Metro stations (DMCC and Sobha Realty) sit on the Red Line, and the Dubai Tram runs along the waterfront connecting the marina to JBR and Media City. Proximity to the beach at JBR and the leisure offering of The Beach and Marina Mall makes it consistently popular with young professionals, couples, and international investors.

Property prices in 2026

Dubai Marina is a mature secondary market, which means there are few off-plan launches within the marina itself. Most transactions are resale. Prices vary significantly by building quality, floor level, and view orientation (marina-facing vs. sea-facing vs. city-facing).

Service charges in Dubai Marina range from AED 12 to AED 22 per sq ft annually depending on the building. Factor this into any yield calculation — a lower purchase price in a high-service-charge building can underperform a pricier unit with lower running costs.

Rental yields

Dubai Marina has consistently delivered gross rental yields of 6–8% for standard apartments, which compares favourably with mature markets in London (3–4%) or Singapore (3–5%). The area's strong and diverse tenant base — including professionals working in Dubai Internet City, Media City, and JLT — keeps vacancy rates low.

Short-term rental (holiday home) is also active in Marina, particularly in buildings with beach or marina views. DTCM holiday home permits are required to operate legally in this segment. Gross short-term rental yields can reach 10–12% in peak months (October–April), but net returns after platform fees, management, and periods of vacancy narrow the gap with long-term rentals. Most investors without existing management infrastructure are better served by a one-year tenancy agreement.

Buying in Dubai Marina

Dubai Marina is a freehold area, meaning any nationality can purchase property with full ownership rights and no time limit. The purchase process follows the standard Dubai freehold process:

  1. Select a property — work with a RERA-registered broker to identify units, view resale options, and compare building quality and service charges.
  2. Sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) — the sales agreement between buyer and seller, usually Form F. Typically includes a 10% deposit.
  3. Apply for No Objection Certificate (NOC) — the developer (usually Emaar or the building owner) must confirm there are no outstanding service charges or disputes on the unit.
  4. Transfer at the Dubai Land Department (DLD) — the title deed is issued in the buyer's name. Transfer fee is 4% of the purchase price, paid to the DLD.
  5. Mortgage — if financing, an UAE bank mortgage can fund up to 80% for residents (75% for non-residents) on a property valued under AED 5M. The bank requires a property valuation report.

The full process typically takes 4–8 weeks from signing the MOU to receiving the title deed, longer if a mortgage is involved. Most Marina transactions complete without legal issues, but we recommend using a registered agency and having a solicitor review the NOC before transferring funds.

Properties purchased at AED 2M or above qualify the buyer for a 10-year UAE Golden Visa. Dubai Marina has a large volume of transactions at this threshold, making it one of the most efficient areas for combined investment and residency planning. Read our full Golden Visa property guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners buy property in Dubai Marina?
Yes. Dubai Marina is a designated freehold area, which means any nationality can purchase property with full ownership rights and no expiry. No UAE residency is required to buy. You will need a valid passport and funds in a UAE bank account to complete the transfer at the Dubai Land Department.
What is the average price per sq ft in Dubai Marina?
Resale apartments in Dubai Marina range from approximately AED 1,500 to AED 2,400 per sq ft as of 2026. The spread reflects differences in building quality, floor level, view, and age. Marina-facing and sea-facing units on high floors in well-maintained buildings sit at the top of the range. Older buildings and low-floor, internal-facing units are at the entry end.
What rental yields does Dubai Marina offer?
Gross rental yields in Dubai Marina typically range from 6% to 8% annually for standard 1- and 2-bedroom apartments on one-year leases. Short-term (holiday home) rentals can yield more in peak season but require a DTCM permit, active management, and carry vacancy risk in the quieter summer months. Net yields after service charges and management fees should be modelled carefully before assuming holiday-home returns.
Does buying in Dubai Marina qualify for a Golden Visa?
Yes, if the property purchase price is AED 2 million or above, the buyer (and their spouse and dependent children) qualify for a 10-year UAE Golden Visa. The AED 2M threshold applies to the purchase price, not a mortgage value — the full amount must be paid. Many 2-bedroom and above transactions in Dubai Marina fall at or above this threshold.
What are the additional costs when buying in Dubai Marina?
The main additional costs are: DLD transfer fee (4% of purchase price), DLD registration trustee fee (approximately AED 4,000–5,000), agency commission (typically 2% of purchase price), NOC fee (charged by the developer, usually AED 500–5,000 depending on the tower), and a mortgage arrangement fee if financing (typically 1% of loan value). Budget 5–7% of the purchase price for all transaction costs combined.
