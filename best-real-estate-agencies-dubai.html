How to choose a real estate agency in Dubai

Dubai's real estate market is one of the fastest-moving in the world. Agencies specialise — some are built around off-plan launches from major developers, others focus on secondary-market resales, and others on the luxury segment or property management. Using the wrong type of agency for your transaction is one of the most common mistakes buyers make.

The most useful signals when choosing an agency:

  • RERA registration — every legitimate agency in Dubai must hold a current RERA licence. Verify this through the DLD licensed broker register before signing anything.
  • Specialisation match — an agency that does 90% off-plan will not give you the same resale experience as one that focuses on secondary market transactions.
  • Review volume and recency — Google review counts reflect transaction volume. An agency with 10,000+ reviews has processed a large number of real transactions. Read recent reviews, not just the average.
  • Area coverage — not every agency covers every community. Some agencies know Dubai Marina and JBR inside out; others focus on inland communities like Mirdif or Dubailand. Ask which areas the agent personally specialises in.

How to verify an agency before signing anything

Do not sign a Form A (listing agreement) or pay any fees before verifying the agency's RERA registration is current. Registration can lapse — a business card and an office do not confirm active licensing.

Steps to take before committing to any Dubai real estate agency:

  1. Check RERA registration — Search the DLD licensed broker register. Verify both the agency's corporate registration and the individual broker's ORN (Office Registration Number).
  2. Read recent Google reviews — Sort by Most Recent, not Top. Look for consistent themes across multiple reviews, both positive and negative.
  3. Ask about fee structure upfront — Standard agency commission in Dubai is 2% of the purchase price (for buyers). Some agencies charge additional admin fees. Get the fee structure in writing before viewing any property.
  4. Confirm Form F will be used — Form F is the official MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) provided by the DLD. It protects both buyer and seller. Be cautious of agencies who use their own contract templates instead.
  5. Meet the actual agent, not just the agency — In large agencies, agent quality varies significantly. The individual agent who handles your transaction matters more than the brand name above the door.
