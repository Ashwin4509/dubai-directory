What is the UAE Golden Visa?

The UAE Golden Visa is a long-term residency permit that was introduced in 2019 and significantly expanded in 2022. It grants the holder the right to live, work and study in the UAE for a renewable 10-year period, without requiring a UAE national as a sponsor and without needing to renew every two or three years like a standard employment visa.

The Golden Visa is issued by the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security (ICP) — the federal authority that handles UAE residency. Several categories qualify: investors, entrepreneurs, specialised talent, outstanding students and researchers. For most foreign property buyers, the real estate investor route is the most direct path.

Unlike an employment visa, the Golden Visa is not tied to a job or employer. Holders can leave the UAE for extended periods (typically up to six months at a stretch without losing status, though the standard condition is at least one visit per 180 days), and can sponsor their spouse, children and, in some cases, parents.

Key change in 2022: The property threshold for the Golden Visa was lowered from AED 5,000,000 to AED 2,000,000, opening the route to a far larger group of buyers. This is now one of the most popular pathways to UAE residency for foreign nationals.

The property requirement: AED 2 million minimum

To qualify for a Golden Visa through property ownership, you must hold property in the UAE with a minimum value of AED 2,000,000 (approximately USD 545,000 at current rates). The key rules are:

  • The property must be registered in your own name with the Dubai Land Department (DLD) — it cannot be held through a company or trust.
  • The AED 2M threshold applies to the total portfolio value, so you can combine multiple properties to reach it — for example, two apartments each valued at AED 1,000,000.
  • The property must be in a completed state (ready to hand over or already handed over) unless it is off-plan from an approved developer — see below for off-plan rules.
  • The property must be in a freehold zone where foreign ownership is permitted.
  • DLD-registered value is used, not a self-declared or agent-estimated value. A DLD valuation certificate is issued at the time of registration.
Note on market value vs. DLD value: Properties are typically registered at the agreed purchase price. If prices have risen since your purchase, your property may be worth more on the open market than its DLD-registered value. In practice, the DLD value (what you paid) is what counts for the visa threshold — not a current market appraisal.

Cash purchase vs. mortgaged property

This is one of the most commonly misunderstood aspects of the property Golden Visa. The rules differ depending on how you purchased:

Cash purchase

If you bought the property without a mortgage, you can apply for the Golden Visa immediately after DLD registration, provided the property value meets or exceeds AED 2,000,000. There is no waiting period and no equity requirement beyond the total value.

Mortgaged property

If your property is mortgaged, the Golden Visa rules require that the equity portion you have paid meets the AED 2,000,000 minimum — not the total property value. A bank letter confirming the paid-up equity is required as part of the application.

In practical terms, this means: if you purchased a property at AED 3,000,000 with a 40% down payment (AED 1,200,000), you would not yet qualify because your equity is below AED 2,000,000. You would need to pay down the mortgage until your equity portion reaches AED 2,000,000.

Off-plan property and the Golden Visa

Off-plan properties — those purchased from a developer before or during construction — can qualify for the Golden Visa, but with an important caveat: the property must be purchased from a developer approved under the ICP's property investor visa scheme, and the amount paid to the developer (instalments received to date) must equal or exceed AED 2,000,000.

This means buying a AED 2,500,000 off-plan unit with a 20% down payment (AED 500,000 paid) would not immediately qualify — you would need to wait until your payments reach AED 2,000,000.

There is an exception for some developers who have pre-approval arrangements. If the total contract value is AED 2M+ and the unit is under construction, some developers can issue a letter confirming the purchase price and the buyer can apply during construction with that documentation. Confirm this directly with the developer and with a UAE immigration consultant before relying on it.

Who qualifies — and who is excluded

The property-based Golden Visa is open to any nationality. There are no restrictions by country of origin. The key eligibility requirements are:

  • Minimum age: 18
  • Valid passport (minimum 6 months remaining)
  • No criminal record in the UAE or home country
  • Property ownership as described above (AED 2M+ in UAE freehold zone)
  • Health insurance coverage in the UAE (a condition of the visa being issued)

The holder's spouse and children (up to age 25 for children) can be sponsored on the same Golden Visa. First-degree relatives (parents) may also be sponsored in some cases. A Gold Visa holder can also sponsor a domestic worker.

Note that the property-based Golden Visa does not automatically grant the right to work in the UAE. If you intend to work for a UAE employer, you will still need a work permit through that employer. However, Golden Visa holders can set up a sole proprietorship or free zone company without a separate residency requirement.

The application process: step by step

1

Obtain a DLD property ownership certificate

A Title Deed is issued by the Dubai Land Department at the time of purchase. If you purchased previously and need a fresh certificate, you can obtain one through the DLD service centres or the Dubai REST app.

2

Get a property valuation from DLD (if required)

For some applications — especially where you are combining multiple properties or if the property was purchased some years ago — ICP may ask for a current DLD valuation certificate. These are issued by the DLD's Valuation section for a small fee.

3

Obtain a bank letter (mortgaged properties only)

If your property is mortgaged, request a letter from your bank confirming the total paid-up equity. This must be on bank letterhead and dated within 30 days of your application.

4

Submit the application via ICP Smart Services

Apply through the ICP portal (icp.gov.ae) or the ICP UAE app. Select "Long-Term Residency" and then "Real Estate Investor." Upload all required documents and pay the application fees online.

5

Medical test and Emirates ID

Once the application is approved in principle, you will be asked to undergo a medical fitness test at a MOHAP-approved centre and have your biometrics taken for an Emirates ID. This applies to all residency visas in the UAE.

6

Receive your Golden Visa stamp and Emirates ID

Once the medical test is clear, your passport is stamped with the 10-year Golden Visa and your Emirates ID is issued. The Emirates ID is required for most day-to-day services in the UAE, including opening a bank account, getting a driving licence and registering utilities.

Documents required

The exact document list can vary slightly based on individual circumstances, but the standard set for a property-based Golden Visa application is:

  • Passport copy (colour, all pages, minimum 6 months validity)
  • Recent passport photograph (white background, meeting UAE specifications)
  • DLD Title Deed or ownership certificate for each qualifying property
  • DLD property valuation certificate (if requested)
  • Bank letter confirming paid-up equity (mortgaged properties only)
  • Developer NOC or payment confirmation letter (off-plan properties)
  • Health insurance policy (valid UAE health insurance is mandatory)
  • Entry stamp or current UAE residency (if already in the UAE)

All foreign-language documents must be attested and officially translated into Arabic or English. If your documents are issued outside the UAE, they may need legalisation (apostille or embassy attestation) before they are accepted.

Benefits of the Golden Visa

The Golden Visa offers a materially different lifestyle security compared to a standard UAE residency visa:

  • 10-year renewable residency — no annual or biennial renewals, no need to remain employed in the UAE to keep your status.
  • No UAE national sponsor required — you hold the visa in your own right.
  • Sponsor immediate family — spouse, children and domestic workers can all be included on your visa.
  • Can stay outside the UAE for up to 6 months without triggering automatic visa cancellation (standard employment visas lapse after 180 days outside the country).
  • Open a UAE bank account independently, without needing a job offer letter or a salary-based relationship.
  • UAE driving licence — with a 10-year Emirates ID, some nationalities qualify for direct licence conversion without retesting.
  • School enrolment for children — your children can be enrolled in UAE government schools (where spaces exist) or benefit from stable long-term planning for private school enrolment.
  • Business activity — Golden Visa holders can sponsor a sole proprietorship on the mainland or register a free zone company without needing a UAE national as a shareholder (for activities where that was previously required).

Renewal and conditions

The Golden Visa is renewable every 10 years, provided you continue to meet the qualifying criteria. For the property-based visa, this means you must still own property in the UAE at or above the AED 2,000,000 threshold at the time of renewal.

If you sell the qualifying property before the 10-year period ends, your Golden Visa does not automatically cancel immediately — but you should replace the qualifying property within a reasonable period to maintain eligibility for renewal. Holding only a below-threshold property at renewal time will result in the visa not being renewed.

There is no annual reporting obligation — you do not need to file property tax returns or report rental income to the immigration authority. However, maintaining valid UAE health insurance is required throughout the visa period.

Tax note: The UAE has a 9% federal corporate tax (effective from 2023) and a 5% VAT, but there is no personal income tax, no capital gains tax and no property tax in Dubai or the wider UAE. Rental income from your Dubai property is not taxed by the UAE (though it may be taxable in your home country — check with a cross-border tax adviser).
