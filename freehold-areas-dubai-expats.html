Freehold Areas in Dubai: Where Expats Can Buy Property (2026)

Updated October 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory editorial team

Foreign nationals — including expats living in Dubai and overseas buyers — can own property outright in designated freehold areas. There are now over 60 such areas, covering everything from entry-level apartments in JVC to beachfront villas on the Palm. This guide explains where you can buy, what ownership means in practice, and which agencies work these areas.

Freehold vs leasehold: what the difference means

In a freehold area, you own the property and the land it sits on outright — in perpetuity, with no expiry. This is equivalent to freehold ownership in the UK or fee simple ownership in the US. You can sell, mortgage, rent, or pass the property to heirs without restriction.

Leasehold is different: you own the property for a fixed term (typically 10 to 99 years) but not the underlying land. At the end of the term, ownership reverts to the freeholder. Leasehold is less common in Dubai than in some other markets, but exists in certain older developments. Most international buyers should target freehold areas.

The Dubai Land Department (DLD) publishes a list of designated freehold areas. All properties in these areas can be purchased by any nationality without restriction.

Property ownership and visa eligibility

Owning freehold property in Dubai can make you eligible for a UAE residency visa, depending on the value of the property:

  • 2-year investor visa: For properties valued at AED 750,000 or more (minimum equity, not mortgage).
  • 10-year Golden Visa: For properties valued at AED 2,000,000 or more (minimum equity). The Golden Visa allows you, your spouse, and dependent children to live in the UAE. It does not require a UAE employer.

The property must be freehold and the title deed must be in your name. Mortgaged properties are eligible provided the equity (your paid-in contribution) meets the threshold — not the total property value. Off-plan properties can qualify once the unit is registered at the DLD, but the visa is typically issued after handover.

Visa applications go through the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA) or via the ICP portal. Most established agencies will refer you to a UAE immigration consultancy that handles this process — it is straightforward for most buyers.

The buying process

Dubai's property buying process is relatively fast compared to most markets. From offer acceptance to title deed transfer, the typical timeline is two to four weeks for cash purchases and four to eight weeks where a mortgage is involved.

  1. Agree on price and terms. The agent prepares a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU/Form F), signed by both parties. The buyer pays a 10% deposit (held in trust) at this stage.
  2. NOC from the developer. For properties in strata communities (apartments, most freehold areas), the seller obtains a No Objection Certificate from the developer confirming no outstanding service charges. This typically takes 5–14 working days.
  3. Title deed transfer at DLD. Both parties (or their authorised representatives) attend the DLD office or an authorised trustee office. Transfer fees are 4% of the purchase price, paid to the DLD. The buyer also pays a registration fee of AED 580 for apartments or AED 430 for off-plan.
  4. Title deed issued. The DLD issues the title deed (usually same day). For mortgaged purchases, the bank holds the original title deed.

The agent's commission is paid by the seller in most cases (2% is the market rate). As a buyer, you should clarify with your agent whether any buyer's fee applies before you engage their services.
