How to Buy Property in Dubai: Complete Guide for Foreign Buyers (2026)

Updated October 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory

Foreigners can legally buy freehold property in designated areas of Dubai. The process is well-regulated, there is no property tax, and ownership gives you a route to a UAE residency visa. This guide walks you through every step — from choosing the right community to registering with the Dubai Land Department.

Can foreigners buy property in Dubai?

Yes. Since 2002, the UAE government has allowed foreigners to purchase freehold property in designated areas of Dubai. These are called freehold zones, and they cover most of the communities that foreign buyers are interested in — Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, Palm Jumeirah, JVC, Business Bay, Dubai Hills Estate and many more.

There is no restriction by nationality. Buyers from any country can purchase. You do not need to be a UAE resident to buy, and you do not need UAE company sponsorship. You own the property in your own name, and you can sell, lease or leave it to heirs without restriction.

Outside the designated freehold zones, foreigners are limited to 99-year leasehold arrangements in certain areas. Most buyers focus on freehold zones where full ownership is available.

Key point: Freehold ownership in Dubai is genuine freehold — you own the land and the unit. There is no ground rent, no leasehold term to worry about, and no requirement to renew ownership. This is different to some other markets where "freehold" carries restrictions.

How much does buying property in Dubai cost?

The total cost of buying property in Dubai is typically 6–8% above the purchase price for secondary market transactions. The largest single charge is the Dubai Land Department (DLD) transfer fee of 4%, paid at the point of registration.

For a secondary market purchase at AED 1,500,000, total buying costs typically land around AED 90,000–110,000 (6–7.5%). For cash buyers this is lower as there is no mortgage registration fee. For off-plan purchases, some developers offer to cover the DLD fee as part of their promotion — worth asking about when buying off-plan.

No property tax: Dubai has no annual property tax, no capital gains tax on property sales, and no inheritance tax. The ongoing cost of ownership is the annual service charge (paid to the building's management), which covers maintenance, security and shared facilities.

The buying process: 6 steps

1

Choose your property and agree the price

Work with a RERA-registered agency to find the right property and negotiate the price. Get confirmation of the asking price, any inclusions and the condition of the property in writing before proceeding.

2

Sign a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

The MOU (Form F in Dubai) is a standard contract that sets out the agreed price, payment schedule, completion date and deposit terms. A deposit of 10% is typical, held by the agency or a registered escrow. Read this carefully — it is binding.

3

The seller obtains a No Objection Certificate (NOC)

The developer must issue an NOC confirming there are no outstanding service charges or disputes on the property. The seller arranges and pays for this. It typically takes 1–5 working days and costs AED 500–5,000 depending on the developer.

4

Transfer at a Dubai Land Department trustee office

Once the NOC is issued, both buyer and seller attend a DLD-registered trustee office (there are several across Dubai) to complete the transfer. Bring your passport, the MOU, the NOC and the balance of the purchase price by manager's cheque. The DLD fee of 4% is paid at this stage.

5

Receive your Title Deed

The trustee office processes the transfer and issues a Title Deed in your name. This is your proof of ownership, registered on the Dubai Land Department's system. The process takes a few hours at the trustee office on the day of transfer.

6

Register with utilities and DEWA

After transfer, register the property with DEWA (Dubai Electricity and Water Authority) in your name. If the property is in a building with a facilities management company, notify them of the ownership change. Your agency should guide you through this.

How long does it take? A straightforward secondary market transaction typically completes in 30–60 days from offer acceptance to Title Deed. The timeline depends mainly on mortgage approval (if applicable), the developer's NOC turnaround time, and buyer/seller availability at the trustee office.

Getting a mortgage as a foreigner

Foreign nationals can get mortgages in Dubai from UAE banks. The key terms to know:

  • Loan-to-Value (LTV): Non-UAE nationals can borrow up to 75% of a property's value for properties below AED 5 million, and 65% above that. UAE nationals get slightly higher LTV ratios. This means a foreign buyer needs a minimum 25% deposit on most properties.
  • Maximum term: 25 years, subject to the mortgage being repaid by age 65 (employed) or 70 (self-employed).
  • Interest rates: Variable rates in Dubai are typically linked to EIBOR (Emirates Interbank Offered Rate). Fixed-rate periods of 1–5 years are available, after which the loan switches to variable.
  • Eligible income: Banks accept salary income, business income and rental income from other properties. Most banks require a minimum monthly salary of AED 15,000 for residents; requirements vary for non-resident buyers.

UAE banks that commonly provide mortgages to foreign buyers include Emirates NBD, Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank (ADCB), Mashreq, HSBC UAE and First Abu Dhabi Bank. A mortgage broker can compare rates across lenders and handle the application — their fee is typically 1% of the loan amount, paid by the bank.

Which community is right for you?

The right community depends entirely on why you're buying — investment, primary residence or holiday home — and your budget. Here is a quick orientation:

For investors seeking rental yield (AED 500K–2M budget)

Jumeirah Village Circle (JVC), Dubai Sports City, Business Bay and Arjan offer some of the highest rental yields in Dubai — typically 6–9% gross — at accessible price points. Supply is high, so tenant demand is driven by value: professional couples and young families who want a good apartment at fair rent.

For primary residence — families

Arabian Ranches, The Springs, Jumeirah Golf Estates and Dubai Hills Estate are Dubai's most popular family communities. Villas with private gardens, good schools nearby (Gems, Repton, Cranleigh), and a genuine neighbourhood feel. Prices range from AED 2M for a smaller villa in The Springs to AED 10M+ for a large Arabian Ranches villa.

For primary residence — urban professionals

Dubai Marina, JBR (Jumeirah Beach Residence) and Downtown Dubai offer the lifestyle that many expats expect when relocating: waterfront walks, restaurants, the beach, metro access. Apartments range from AED 900K for a studio to AED 5M+ for a large-format Marina apartment.

For ultra-luxury and capital growth

Palm Jumeirah signature villas, Emirates Hills, Jumeirah Bay Island and Mohammed Bin Rashid City represent the top of the Dubai market. These are long-term holdings where capital appreciation and exclusivity matter more than rental yield.

Off-plan vs secondary market: which should you buy?

This is the most common question foreign buyers ask, and the answer depends on your situation.

Off-plan (buying before completion)

  • Advantages: Lower entry price, flexible payment plans (often 50–70% paid during construction, balance on completion), potential capital appreciation before handover, some developers waive the 4% DLD fee
  • Disadvantages: You can't inspect the finished product, there is construction risk (delays, quality issues), you won't receive rent until handover, and your money is tied up for 2–4 years
  • Best for: Investors with longer horizons, buyers who want lower monthly outgoings during construction, and buyers willing to accept completion risk in exchange for price

Secondary market (buying a completed property)

  • Advantages: You see exactly what you're buying, rental income starts immediately, you can get a mortgage, the area is established so you know the community
  • Disadvantages: Higher upfront cost, full DLD fee payable at transfer, may need renovation
  • Best for: Primary residence buyers, buyers who need mortgage financing, and investors who want immediate rental income
Developer risk with off-plan: In Dubai, off-plan payments must be held in an escrow account regulated by RERA. This protects your deposit if the developer runs into difficulty. Check that the project you're buying is registered with RERA and that the escrow account exists before paying any deposit.

Property and UAE residency visas

Property ownership in Dubai gives you a route to UAE residency. The two main routes:

  • 2-year investor visa: Available to property owners with a property valued at AED 750,000 or more. Renewable every 2 years, requires you to visit the UAE at least once per 180 days to maintain residency.
  • 10-year Golden Visa: Available to property owners with property valued at AED 2 million or more. The property can be mortgaged but the equity must be at least AED 2 million. The Golden Visa allows you to sponsor your spouse and children, and does not require you to physically reside in the UAE.

Both visas are investor residency — they do not grant citizenship or permanent residency in the traditional sense, but they give you legal UAE residency status and the right to open UAE bank accounts, get a UAE driving licence and access UAE healthcare and education.

How to choose a real estate agency

Every agency acting in a Dubai property transaction must be RERA-registered. This is the minimum baseline. Beyond that, choosing the right agency means matching your needs to the agency's strengths:

  • If you're buying for investment: Choose an agency with deep knowledge of your target community — not just general Dubai coverage. Ask them specifically: what have they sold in JVC / Marina / wherever you're looking in the last 6 months, what yields are their clients achieving, and can they refer you to past investor clients.
  • If you're buying for primary residence as a family: Choose an agency that knows the schools, the communities, the commute times. Hamptons, Allsopp & Allsopp, Espace Real Estate and Betterhomes all have strong family-residential track records.
  • If you're buying luxury or ultra-luxury: Knight Frank, Savills, Luxhabitat Sotheby's and Engel & Völkers all have the brand credibility and off-market access appropriate for the top of the market.

Checking an agency's Google Reviews is a useful signal. A 4.5+ rating with hundreds of reviews suggests consistent performance. Be wary of agencies with very few reviews or a sudden spike in 5-star reviews.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying a deposit before the MOU is signed. Never transfer money to a seller or agent without a signed, witnessed MOU in place.
  • Not verifying the agent's RERA registration. Check the agent's card number on the DLD's smart app (Dubai REST) before working with them.
  • Buying off-plan without checking the developer's track record. Ask about previous handovers — were they on time? What was the quality? How responsive is the developer to snagging issues?
  • Underestimating the true cost. The 4% DLD fee plus 2% agency commission adds up. Budget for 7–8% above the purchase price for all transaction costs.
  • Ignoring the service charge. Service charges (annual maintenance fees for the building) vary enormously — from AED 8 per square foot in a budget building to AED 40+ in premium towers. A high service charge can significantly reduce your net rental yield.
  • Not getting independent legal advice. For large purchases, especially off-plan from developers you don't know, a UAE-licensed property lawyer reviewing the Sale and Purchase Agreement is worth the AED 3,000–8,000 it costs.
  • Assuming all areas are freehold. Confirm that the specific area and project is in a freehold zone before agreeing to anything. Your agent should confirm this upfront, but you can also verify on the DLD website.
