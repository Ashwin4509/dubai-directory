How to Find a RERA-Registered Agent in Dubai

Updated November 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory

Every legitimate real estate agent in Dubai must hold a RERA card — a licence issued by the Dubai Land Department confirming they are authorised to conduct property transactions. Checking it takes 60 seconds and is the single most important step you can take before signing anything or handing over money.

What is RERA and why does it matter?

RERA — the Real Estate Regulatory Agency — is the government body within the Dubai Land Department responsible for regulating the property market. It was established in 2007 following Dubai's first major property boom and introduced to bring structure, transparency, and legal protection to what had been a loosely regulated market.

Among its functions, RERA manages the licensing and training of all real estate brokers operating in Dubai. To obtain a RERA card, an agent must:

  • Pass the Certified Training for Real Estate Brokers (CTRB) exam administered by DREI (Dubai Real Estate Institute)
  • Be sponsored by a registered real estate brokerage firm
  • Hold a valid UAE residence visa
  • Renew their card annually

An agent without a valid RERA card cannot legally represent a buyer or seller in a property transaction in Dubai. They cannot sign Form A (listing agreement), Form B (buyer's agreement), or Form F (Memorandum of Understanding). Any such documentation signed by an unlicensed agent is legally invalid and unenforceable.

The practical risk: Unlicensed agents typically operate at the fringes — on WhatsApp broadcast lists, through informal referrals, or as "assistants" to licensed agents. They may request deposits, booking fees, or reservation amounts before the transaction reaches the DLD. If you pay money to an unlicensed operator and the deal falls through, you have little legal recourse.

How to verify RERA registration (step by step)

  1. Go to dubailand.gov.ae Navigate to the Dubai Land Department's official website. Look for the eServices section or use the search function for "Licensed Real Estate Brokers."
  2. Open the licensed brokers search The DLD maintains a live, searchable database of all currently licensed brokers. It is updated regularly and shows active and expired registrations.
  3. Search by name or RERA card number Enter the agent's full name as shown on their business card or ID, or their RERA card number if they have provided it. You can also search by the name of the brokerage firm.
  4. Check the registration status and expiry date A valid result will show the agent as "Active" and display the card's expiry date. If the card is expired — even by one day — the agent is not currently licensed to transact. If the name does not appear at all, they are not registered.
  5. Verify the brokerage they are registered under The agent's RERA card is tied to a specific brokerage. Confirm they are currently employed by the firm they claim to represent — agents who have recently changed employers may have a card in transition.

What the RERA card tells you

The RERA card itself carries the agent's name, photo, card number, issuing brokerage, and expiry date. A physical card is what a licensed agent will show you when you request verification. The DLD database is the definitive check — the physical card can be forged, the database cannot.

Beyond basic licensing, the RERA card does not tell you:

  • The agent's experience or track record
  • Whether they specialise in your property type or area
  • Their Google reviews or client satisfaction history
  • Whether their brokerage has been subject to RERA disciplinary action

RERA registration is a floor, not a quality guarantee. Use it as the minimum threshold before you even consider working with someone, then conduct your own due diligence on the agent and their firm.

Red flags to watch for

These are common patterns associated with unlicensed or problematic agents in Dubai:

  • Refuses to provide RERA card number. Any licensed agent should produce their RERA number instantly — it is on their business card, email signature, and marketing materials. Hesitation or avoidance is a serious red flag.
  • Requests a cash deposit before signing any RERA form. Legitimate transactions proceed through formal RERA documentation. A request for cash before any paperwork is a warning sign.
  • Claims the property is "off-market" and available only through them. Off-market deals exist, but are more commonly used as a pressure tactic to bypass proper procedures.
  • Cannot or will not provide the developer's escrow account details for off-plan. All off-plan payments in Dubai must go into a RERA-registered escrow account, not to the agent or directly to the developer's general account.
  • Pushes you to sign quickly before you can verify details. Urgency pressure is almost always a tactic to prevent proper due diligence.
  • Works from a personal WhatsApp number with no registered brokerage branding. Individual agents operating outside a registered brokerage cannot hold a RERA card.

RERA forms and your legal protection

RERA has standardised the documentation for property transactions in Dubai. Understanding these forms is part of protecting yourself:

Form A — Listing Agreement (Seller)

Signed between the seller and their agent, authorising the agent to market the property. A property should not be listed for sale by an agent unless they have a signed Form A. Buyers sometimes request to see the listing agent's Form A to confirm they have authority to sell.

Form B — Buyer's Agency Agreement

An agreement between the buyer and their agent. It is not always used in practice but protects both parties by clarifying the commission arrangement and the agent's authority to represent the buyer.

Form F — Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

The key transaction document, signed between buyer and seller at the point of agreement on price and terms. It records the agreed price, deposit amount (typically 10% of purchase price, held in trust), completion date, and conditions. The Form F triggers the buyer's NOC application from the developer and the DLD transfer process.

Form I — Agency Authorisation

Signed when a seller wishes to list their property with multiple agencies simultaneously. It records each agency's authority and prevents disputes over commission when more than one agent is involved.

Questions to ask before you appoint an agent

These questions will quickly tell you whether an agent is worth your time:

  1. "Can I have your RERA card number?" — The first question. Any hesitation is a red flag.
  2. "How many transactions have you personally completed in [your target area] in the last 12 months?" — Area expertise is specific. An agent active in JVC may know nothing useful about Palm Jumeirah.
  3. "Which developers do you have direct relationships with for off-plan?" — For off-plan buyers, developer relationships determine access to launch pricing and unit selection.
  4. "How will you communicate with me and how often?" — Sets expectations on responsiveness and reporting.
  5. "What is your commission and how is it paid?" — For secondary market, clarify if the buyer pays commission (standard 2%), or if it is split with the listing agent. For off-plan, commission is usually developer-paid.

What to do if something goes wrong

If you believe you have been defrauded or dealt with dishonestly by a real estate agent in Dubai, you have several routes:

  • RERA complaint portal — File a formal complaint against a licensed agent or brokerage at dubailand.gov.ae. RERA has the authority to suspend or cancel licences.
  • Dubai Courts — For financial disputes involving significant amounts, the Dubai Courts will hear property-related cases. Small Claims Court handles disputes up to AED 500,000.
  • Dubai Police economic crime unit — For suspected fraud involving unlicensed agents or missing deposits, Dubai Police's economic crime unit handles property fraud cases.
  • RERA's Real Estate Dispute Settlement Centre (RDSC) — Handles disputes between buyers, sellers, and agents, often as a faster alternative to the courts.
