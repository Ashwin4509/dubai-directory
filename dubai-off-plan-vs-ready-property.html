Off-Plan vs Ready Property in Dubai: Which Is Right for You?

Updated November 2026 · Dubai Real Estate Directory

Off-plan accounts for over 60% of Dubai's residential transactions. Ready property accounts for the rest — and in many ways, the two are completely different purchases. The price gap, the risks, the payment mechanics, and the buyer profile are all different. Here is what you need to know before choosing.

What off-plan and ready mean in Dubai

Off-plan property is a unit purchased from a developer before the building is completed — often before construction has even started. You are buying a legal commitment to a unit that will exist in the future. Payment is spread over a construction-linked or time-based schedule, and you take ownership on completion.

Ready property is an existing, completed unit purchased in the secondary market (from an individual owner) or directly from a developer as a completed stock unit. You can inspect it, transfer title on Day 1, and move in or lease it out immediately.

These are fundamentally different transactions — different risks, different documentation, different financing options, and different timelines. Most buyers in Dubai end up in one camp or the other based on their primary goal: capital appreciation and payment flexibility (off-plan) versus immediate income and certainty (ready).

Pricing: off-plan discount or premium?

The conventional wisdom is that off-plan is cheaper than ready. This is no longer reliably true in 2026 — and in many cases the opposite is true.

In the 2020–2022 period, developers launched at 20–30% below comparable secondary market prices to attract buyers. That spread has largely closed. Today, developers in popular areas often price launches at or above secondary market comparables, banking on the payment plan flexibility and brand premium to justify the price.

Where off-plan pricing genuinely provides value:

  • Early launch phases of new masterplan communities (before the neighbourhood is proven)
  • Locations where the developer has acquired land cheaply and is passing through lower costs
  • New areas with lower secondary market activity (less price discovery, so developers can undercut)

Where off-plan pricing is not competitive:

  • High-profile launches by marquee developers (Emaar, Meraas, Sobha) in established areas
  • Luxury branded residences where the brand premium is built into the launch price
  • Second or third phases of proven communities, where the developer knows demand is high
Always compare. Before committing to an off-plan launch price, check what comparable ready units in the same area are trading at on the DLD's real estate transactions database. If off-plan is priced at par or above ready, the payment plan benefit may not compensate for the wait and risk.

Payment plans — the off-plan advantage

The genuine, structural advantage of off-plan in Dubai is the payment plan. Ready property requires you to pay 100% of the purchase price (or take a mortgage) at transfer. Off-plan allows you to spread payments over 2–5 years.

A typical off-plan payment plan might look like:

  • 10% — booking deposit on signing
  • 10% — on commencement of construction (3–6 months)
  • 40% — in milestone instalments tied to construction progress
  • 40% — on completion and handover

Some developers have offered post-handover payment plans — where 30–40% of the price is paid in instalments after you receive keys. This effectively reduces the capital required at completion and has proven highly attractive to investors who plan to generate rental income to fund the remaining instalments.

The implication for investors is significant: a 2,000 sq ft apartment launching at AED 2.5 million might require only AED 500,000 in the first year, compared to AED 2.5 million (or a full mortgage) for a comparable ready unit. This capital efficiency is why off-plan dominates the investor market.

Risks: completion, delivery, and market timing

Off-plan carries risks that ready property does not:

Completion delay

Dubai off-plan projects are routinely delivered 6–24 months later than the developer's original handover date. The Dubai 2008 market collapse saw hundreds of projects cancelled or indefinitely stalled. RERA's current escrow account requirement — all buyer payments must be held in a DLD-registered escrow account and can only be released to the developer against construction milestones — provides significant protection, but delay risk remains real.

Developer insolvency

Smaller or newer developers carry higher risk. The escrow account regime protects against outright loss, but insolvency proceedings are slow and buyers may be tied up for years. This is a reason to favour established, well-capitalised developers — particularly publicly listed entities like Emaar, which has SEC-equivalent disclosure obligations.

Market timing risk

You are committing to a future price today. If the market falls between purchase and completion, you may complete into a property worth less than you paid, or less than comparable units available at the time of handover.

Specification and finish risk

What is shown in the developer's showroom or marketing renders may differ from what is actually delivered. Material substitutions, layout changes, and specification downgrades can and do happen. The SPA (Sales and Purchase Agreement) governs what you are legally owed, and buyers should read it carefully — particularly the developer's right to make "minor changes."

Ready property risks are different: You risk overpaying in a hot market, you face immediate service charge obligations, and older buildings may have higher maintenance costs. But you have no completion uncertainty and your income starts on Day 1.

Yield and rental income

Ready property wins on immediate yield. The day you transfer title, you can lease the unit and start earning. For income-focused investors, this is a major consideration — particularly given that Dubai mortgage rates and service charges mean carrying a vacant property for 2–3 years during construction is a real cost.

Off-plan investors typically expect capital appreciation at completion to compensate for the wait and delivery risk. If you buy off-plan and the market rises 15–25% by completion, your return on the equity deployed is high. If the market is flat or falls, you have waited 3 years for no income and no capital gain.

Short-term rental (holiday home) strategies require a ready property — you cannot list an off-plan unit on Airbnb or Booking.com. Investors who want to operate a holiday home business from day one must buy ready.

Who each option suits

Off-plan suits

  • Investors with a 3–5 year horizon who are comfortable with completion risk
  • Buyers who cannot mobilise the full purchase price upfront and need to spread payments
  • People planning to relocate to Dubai in 2–3 years who want to lock in a price now
  • Investors seeking capital appreciation in an area where off-plan pricing is genuinely below ready comparables

Ready property suits

  • Owner-occupiers who need to move in immediately or within weeks
  • Investors who want rental income from Month 1
  • Short-term rental operators who want to run a holiday home business
  • Buyers who want price certainty and the ability to inspect what they are buying before committing
  • Those with low risk tolerance who want to avoid completion and delivery uncertainty

Choosing the right developer for off-plan

If you decide off-plan is right for you, the developer you choose is the most important decision. Key factors:

  • Track record of delivery. How many projects has the developer completed on time, and what was the average delay? For newer developers, there is no track record — that is itself the risk.
  • RERA registration and escrow compliance. Every developer must have a RERA-registered project and an escrow account. Verify at dubailand.gov.ae before paying anything.
  • Financial backing. Government-linked or publicly listed developers (Emaar, Meraas, Nakheel) have stronger balance sheets and are less likely to face insolvency. Boutique private developers carry higher risk — higher potential reward, but higher risk.
  • Specific project location. Even strong developers build in weak locations sometimes. Research the community, the master plan, and the surrounding infrastructure before the developer's brand carries you in.
